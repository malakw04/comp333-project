While the Decision Tree achieved the highest accuracy (72.4%), it exhibited very low recall (24.2%), indicating poor performance in identifying rainy days. This suggests that the model is biased toward the majority class (no rain).
In contrast, both Random Forest and XGBoost significantly improved recall to over 55%, more than doubling the baseline performance. This resulted in a substantial increase in F1-score (from 0.34 to ~0.52), indicating a better balance between precision and recall.
Although accuracy decreased slightly, both ensemble models achieved higher AUC scores (~0.70), demonstrating improved overall classification capability. Among them, XGBoost provided the best trade-off, achieving the highest recall and F1-score, making it the most suitable model for predicting rainfall.

In this context, recall is particularly important because failing to predict rain may have more significant consequences than false positives, making ensemble methods more appropriate despite lower accuracy.

Additionally, the AUC improved from 0.70 to 0.74, demonstrating enhanced overall classification performance. These improvements suggest that the engineered features successfully captured temporal dependencies and evolving weather patterns that were not present in the original feature set.
Although overall accuracy did not increase, this is expected due to the trade-off between precision and recall. In this context, improving recall is more important, as failing to predict rainfall is more critical than generating false positives.
Overall, XGBoost emerged as the best-performing model, benefiting the most from the additional feature engineering.

Due to class imbalance in the dataset, accuracy alone is not a reliable metric, as a model can achieve high accuracy by predicting the majority class. Therefore, greater emphasis is placed on F1-score and AUC, which better capture the model’s ability to balance precision and recall and distinguish between classes.

While accuracy is still reported for completeness, it is not the primary evaluation metric due to class imbalance. Instead, F1-score and AUC are emphasized, as they provide a more informative assessment of the model’s ability to correctly identify the minority class (rainfall) while maintaining a balance between false positives and false negatives.

Recall is also particularly important in this context, as failing to predict rainfall may have more significant consequences than predicting rain when it does not occur.

Despite a relatively low silhouette score, the clustering results reveal meaningful and interpretable weather regimes, including dry, cold, and high-precipitation conditions.

PCA was applied to reduce the dimensionality of the engineered feature space while preserving 95% of the variance, resulting in 11 principal components. This indicates that the engineered features contribute meaningful and non-redundant information to the dataset.

KMeans clustering was then applied to the reduced feature space, and the optimal number of clusters was determined to be 3 using silhouette analysis. Although the silhouette score was relatively low (~0.18), this is expected given the continuous and overlapping nature of weather data.

Despite this, the clusters were interpretable and revealed distinct weather regimes. One cluster was characterized by extremely high precipitation levels, representing heavy rainfall conditions. Another cluster corresponded to warm and dry weather, while the third cluster captured colder conditions with moderate precipitation and higher elevation.
These findings demonstrate that, even in the absence of strong cluster separation, unsupervised learning can uncover meaningful patterns in complex environmental data.

The relatively low clustering quality suggests that weather patterns exist on a continuum rather than forming sharply separated groups, highlighting the inherent complexity of atmospheric systems.

The first two principal components are linear combinations of the original engineered features and represent the directions of maximum variance in the dataset.
